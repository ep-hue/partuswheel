Quick answer

Turn the inner disc until its LMP arrow points to the first day of the last menstrual period. The due date is at the 40-week (280-day) mark, and today's gestational age sits next to today's date. Example: LMP March 1, 2026 → EDD December 6, 2026; on June 15, 2026 the pregnancy is 15w1d. Paper wheels are not exact: in a 2014 study only 10 of 31 gave the 280-day due date.

What a pregnancy wheel is

A pregnancy wheel — also called an OB wheel, obstetric wheel, gestation calculator or gestational age wheel — is a pair of concentric discs used to turn one pregnancy date into all the others without arithmetic. It encodes the convention used in clinical dating: EDD = first day of the LMP + 280 days (40 weeks), as described in ACOG Committee Opinion 700. Midwives, labor and delivery nurses, family physicians and nursing students use it at the bedside and in exams; patients often meet it at the first prenatal visit.

The parts of an OB wheel

PartWhat it shows
Outer discA 12-month calendar, one tick per day.
Inner discWeeks of gestation, usually 0 to 42–44, with a tick for each day or each week.
LMP arrow (week 0)The pointer you set on the first day of the last menstrual period.
EDD arrow (week 40)Where the estimated due date is read: 280 days after the LMP arrow.
Conception / ovulation markAround week 2 (day 14) on many wheels. Fetal (conceptional) age is gestational age minus about 2 weeks.
Milestone windowsDepending on the printer: trimester bands, screening windows, and sometimes average fetal length and weight by week.

Printed milestones differ between manufacturers, so check what your wheel marks before relying on them.

How to use it, step by step

From the last menstrual period

  1. Find the first day of the LMP on the outer calendar.
  2. Turn the inner disc until its LMP (week 0) arrow points to that date.
  3. Read the EDD at the 40-week arrow.
  4. Find today's date on the calendar; the inner disc next to it shows the gestational age in weeks and days.
  5. Read other dates the same way: find the week on the inner disc, then read the calendar date beside it.

From a known due date

Set the 40-week arrow on the EDD. The LMP arrow now shows the equivalent LMP, and gestational age on any date reads as above. This is how you continue with a due date set by someone else, for example on a referral.

From an ultrasound

Find the date of the scan on the calendar and turn the disc until the gestational age measured at the scan sits on that date. The EDD arrow then shows the ultrasound-based due date. Whether that date replaces the LMP date is decided by the ACOG thresholds, not by the wheel — see LMP vs ultrasound due date.

Checking against an early scan. An ultrasound on April 20, 2026 measures 7w4d. On the wheel, setting 7w4d on April 20 puts the EDD arrow on December 3, 2026 — 3 days earlier than the LMP date. Before 9w0d, ACOG CO 700 changes the due date only when the difference is more than 5 days, so the LMP due date of December 6 stands. Had the scan measured 8w2d (a difference of 8 days), the EDD would move to the ultrasound date.

Checking against Naegele's rule. March 1 + 7 days − 3 months + 1 year gives December 8, 2026 — 2 days later than the 280-day date, because calendar months differ in length. A wheel set correctly gives December 6. See Naegele's rule for more examples.

Five mistakes that put a wheel off

  1. Setting the wrong day of the period. The arrow goes on the first day of the last menstrual period — not the last day, not the day of a positive test and not the conception date. Setting the conception date on the LMP arrow makes every reading two weeks too early.
  2. Forgetting the 28-day-cycle assumption. The wheel assumes ovulation around day 14. With long or irregular cycles, or an uncertain LMP, LMP dating is less reliable, and a first-trimester ultrasound (up to 13w6d) is the most accurate method (ACOG CO 700).
  3. Leap years. A wheel with a 365-day calendar has no February 29. For a pregnancy that spans it, the wheel reads one day late: LMP December 1, 2027 gives a true EDD of September 6, 2028, but such a wheel points at September 7. In the 2014 study below, only the apps accounted for leap years.
  4. Mixing the dates of an ultrasound. The measured gestational age must be set on the date the scan was done, not on the date you read the report.
  5. Reading the wrong ring. Some wheels print fetal (conceptional) age next to gestational age; the two differ by about 2 weeks. Clinical dating uses gestational age.

How accurate are paper pregnancy wheels?

Less accurate than they look. Chambliss and Clark (American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2014) compared 31 paper gestational age wheels with 20 smartphone apps:

  • Only 10 of the 31 paper wheels gave the standard 280-day due date.
  • The largest error was 4 days, and two wheels disagreed with each other by 7 days.
  • All 20 apps gave the correct 280-day date, and only the apps accounted for leap years.
  • The authors concluded that paper gestational age wheels should be abandoned.

On top of the printing error, a wheel is read by eye, so a day either way is easy to lose. These errors are separate from the uncertainty of the dating method itself — LMP recall and ultrasound each have their own margins.

Common questions

How do you use a pregnancy wheel?

Turn the inner disc so its LMP arrow points to the first day of the last menstrual period. Read the due date at the 40-week mark and the gestational age next to today's date.

Do you use the first or the last day of the period on a pregnancy wheel?

The first day of the last menstrual period. Using the last day of bleeding, a positive test or the conception date shifts every reading.

How accurate is a pregnancy wheel?

In a 2014 study of 31 paper wheels, only 10 gave the standard 280-day due date; the largest error was 4 days. All 20 smartphone apps tested were correct.

Why does my pregnancy wheel give a different due date than Naegele's rule?

Naegele's rule adds calendar months, which differ in length, so it can land up to 3 days after LMP + 280 days. A wheel can also carry its own printing error of a few days.

Can you use a pregnancy wheel with an ultrasound date?

Yes: set the gestational age measured at the scan on the scan date and read the EDD. Whether to adopt it is decided by the ACOG CO 700 redating thresholds.
