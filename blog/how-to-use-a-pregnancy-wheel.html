How to Use a Pregnancy Wheel (OB Wheel), Step by Step

Reading the due date and gestational age from an OB wheel — from the LMP, from a known due date or from an ultrasound — and what a 2014 study found about the accuracy of paper wheels.

Quick answer

Line up the wheel's arrow with the first day of the LMP; read the due date at the 40-week (280-day) mark and today's gestational age next to today's date. To work backwards, set the due date or the ultrasound gestational age on the scan date. Paper wheels are not exact: in a 2014 study only 10 of 31 gave the 280-day due date, and the authors recommended abandoning them in favor of electronic calculators.

What a pregnancy wheel is

A pregnancy wheel — also called an OB wheel, obstetric wheel or gestation calculator — is two concentric discs: an outer calendar and an inner disc marked in weeks of gestation. Turning the inner disc places week 0 on the first day of the LMP, and every other week lines up with its calendar date. It encodes the same convention used in clinical dating: EDD = LMP + 280 days (ACOG CO 700).

How to use it, step by step

From the last menstrual period

  1. Find the first day of the LMP on the outer calendar.
  2. Turn the inner disc until its LMP (or 0-week) arrow points to that date.
  3. Read the EDD at the 40-week arrow.
  4. Find today's date on the calendar; the inner disc shows the gestational age in weeks and days.
  5. Read milestones the same way — most wheels mark the trimesters and common screening windows.

From a known due date

Set the 40-week arrow on the EDD. The LMP arrow now shows the equivalent LMP, and today's gestational age reads as above.

From an ultrasound

Find the date of the scan on the calendar and turn the disc until the gestational age measured at the scan sits on that date. The EDD arrow then shows the ultrasound-based due date. Whether that date should replace the LMP date is decided by the ACOG thresholds — see LMP vs ultrasound due date.

How accurate are paper pregnancy wheels?

Less accurate than they look. Chambliss and Clark (American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2014) compared 31 paper gestational age wheels with 20 smartphone apps:

  • Only 10 of the 31 paper wheels gave the standard 280-day due date.
  • The largest error was 4 days, and two wheels disagreed with each other by 7 days.
  • All 20 apps gave the correct 280-day date, and only the apps accounted for leap years.
  • The authors concluded that paper gestational age wheels should be abandoned.

On top of the printing error, a wheel is read by eye, so a day either way is easy to lose. These errors are separate from the uncertainty of the dating method itself — LMP recall and ultrasound each have their own margins.

Pregnancy wheel vs Naegele's rule vs a calculator

All three aim at LMP + 280 days. Naegele's rule (+7 days, −3 months, +1 year) can land up to 3 days later because calendar months differ in length — see Naegele's rule. A paper wheel adds its own printing error. An electronic calculator counts the days exactly, including leap years.

How do you use a pregnancy wheel?

Can you use a pregnancy wheel with an ultrasound date?

Yes: set the gestational age measured at the scan on the scan date and read the EDD. Whether to adopt it is decided by the ACOG CO 700 redating thresholds.
