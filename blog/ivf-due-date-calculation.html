How to Calculate a Due Date After IVF: Day 3, Day 5, Day 6 and FET

The embryo-age formula from ACOG Committee Opinion 700, a worked example for each transfer day, and why clinics sometimes give a different date for day-6 or day-7 blastocysts.

Quick answer

IVF due date = embryo transfer date + (266 − embryo age in days). That is +263 days for a day-3 embryo and +261 days for a day-5 blastocyst (both given in ACOG CO 700), and +260 days for a day-6 blastocyst by the same arithmetic. Fresh or frozen (FET) makes no difference. Equivalent LMP = due date − 280 days.

Why IVF dating is different

In a spontaneous pregnancy the date of conception is estimated from the LMP. After IVF it is known: the embryo's age on the day of transfer is recorded. ACOG Committee Opinion 700 therefore says the ART-derived gestational age should be used to assign the EDD, using the age of the embryo and the date of transfer.

The formula

A pregnancy lasts about 266 days from fertilization (280 days from an LMP two weeks before conception). On the day of transfer the embryo is already 3, 5 or 6 days old, so:

EDD = transfer date + (266 − embryo age)

Cross-check for a fresh cycle: if the eggs were retrieved and fertilized on March 11 and a day-5 embryo was transferred on March 16, the retrieval date + 266 days is also December 2, 2026.

Frozen embryo transfer (FET)

The same formula applies. What matters is the embryo's developmental age at transfer, not whether it was fresh or thawed: a frozen day-5 blastocyst is dated as day 5.

Day-6 and day-7 blastocysts: why clinics differ

Under the embryo-age method, each extra day of embryo age moves the due date one day earlier: day 5 = +261, day 6 = +260, day 7 = +259. Some fertility clinics instead date all blastocysts transferred on the same day alike — for example treating day-6 and day-7 embryos as day 5 (+261). The difference is only 1–2 days. For the medical record, follow the dating convention of the treating clinic and document which one was used.

Donor eggs and twins

  • Donor eggs or donor embryos: the calculation is the same — it depends on the embryo's age and the transfer date, not on whose egg it was.
  • Twins: the dating arithmetic does not change. The timing of delivery for a twin pregnancy is a separate clinical decision.

Can an ultrasound change an IVF due date?

Generally not. ACOG directs that the ART-derived gestational age be used to assign the EDD, because the conception date is known precisely. The ultrasound redating thresholds are for pregnancies dated by the LMP — see LMP vs ultrasound due date.

Common questions

How do you calculate a due date after IVF?

Add (266 − embryo age) days to the transfer date: +263 days for a day-3 embryo, +261 for a day-5 blastocyst and +260 for a day-6 blastocyst.

How is the due date calculated for a frozen embryo transfer?

Exactly the same way as a fresh transfer, using the embryo's age at transfer. A thawed day-5 blastocyst is dated as day 5: transfer + 261 days.

How many weeks pregnant am I on the day of a day-5 transfer?

2 weeks 5 days. On the day of a day-3 transfer the gestational age is 2 weeks 3 days, and on the day of a day-6 transfer 2 weeks 6 days.

Why did my clinic give the same due date for a day-5 and a day-6 embryo?

Some clinics date all blastocysts transferred on the same day alike. The embryo-age method moves the date one day earlier per day of embryo age. The difference is 1–2 days; follow the treating clinic's convention.

Does an ultrasound change an IVF due date?

Generally not. ACOG says the ART-derived gestational age should be used to assign the EDD.
