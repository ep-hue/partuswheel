Bishop Score Interpretation: What a Score of 2, 4, 6 or 8 Means

What each total means before labor induction, which cut-offs current guidance actually uses, and why parity and local protocol still matter.

Quick answer

The Bishop score runs from 0 to 13; higher means a riper cervix. The most common convention is 6 or less = unfavorable (cervical ripening is usually considered before oxytocin) and 8 or more = favorable (NICE NG207). A score of 7 is borderline. ACOG's 2025 guideline states that there is no universally accepted cut-off and that thresholds from 3 to 8 have been used.

What the Bishop score measures

Edward Bishop described the score in 1964 as a way to judge whether the cervix was ready for elective induction. Five findings from a digital vaginal exam each earn points, and the points are added up:

Not every source draws the line in the same place. The American Academy of Family Physicians review (2022) calls a score above 8 favorable and 5 or less unfavorable, and StatPearls notes that many studies used “>8” for a favorable cervix. ACOG's Clinical Practice Guideline No. 9 (2025) deliberately gives no single number. Whatever cut-off you use, document the total and the five components, not only the label.

What does a Bishop score of 2 mean?

A score of 2 is a very unripe cervix — typically closed or barely open, long, firm or posterior, with a high presenting part. Under any of the conventions above it is unfavorable, so an induction would normally start with cervical ripening rather than oxytocin.

What does a Bishop score of 4 mean?

Still unfavorable. A 4 can be reached in different ways — for example 1–2 cm dilation, 40–50% effacement, station −2 and a mid-position cervix — which is why the components matter as much as the total. Ripening is usually considered first.

Is a Bishop score of 6 favorable?

Under the most common convention, no: 6 is the top of the unfavorable range. NICE NG207 recommends ripening for a score of 6 or less. Two sources of confusion: the simplified Bishop score (three components, 0–9) uses “more than 5” as its favorable threshold, and some induction protocols — for example the California Maternal Quality Care Collaborative, as summarized in StatPearls — use 6 or more as the target for multiparous patients. Make sure everyone is using the same scale.

What does a Bishop score of 7 mean?

Seven sits between the two common cut-offs. NICE treats anything above 6 as suitable for amniotomy and oxytocin, while sources that use “8 or more” as favorable would still call 7 borderline. This is where parity, gestational age and the indication for induction weigh most, and where your unit's protocol decides.

Is a Bishop score of 8 good?

Yes. 8 or more is the most widely used definition of a favorable cervix. StatPearls describes a favorable cervix as one where the likelihood of vaginal delivery after induction is similar to that after spontaneous labor. Scores of 9–13 are more favorable still.

What Bishop score is needed to start oxytocin (Pitocin)?

There is no single number. In NICE NG207 the split is at 6: 6 or less → cervical ripening first; more than 6 → amniotomy and oxytocin. StatPearls cites a California (CMQCC) approach of continuing ripening until the score reaches 6 or more in multiparous and 8 or more in nulliparous patients. ACOG (2025) leaves the threshold to clinical judgment and protocol.

Why parity and protocol still matter

Bishop built the score on multiparous patients; it is now applied to nulliparous patients as well (StatPearls). ACOG (2025) notes that parity and gestational age also influence how an induction goes. The same total therefore carries a different meaning for a first labor than for a fifth.

Limitations

  • The exam is subjective, so two examiners can arrive at different totals.
  • StatPearls notes the score may predict induction failure less well with newer ripening strategies.
  • It should not be used when a digital cervical exam is contraindicated, such as placenta previa or preterm prelabor rupture of membranes (StatPearls).

Common questions

What is a Bishop score?

A 0–13 score of cervical readiness for labor induction, described by Edward Bishop in 1964. It adds points for dilation, effacement, fetal station, consistency and position from a vaginal exam.

What is a good Bishop score?

8 or more is the most common definition of a favorable cervix (NICE NG207). 6 or less is usually called unfavorable. ACOG's 2025 guideline notes there is no universally accepted cut-off.

Is a Bishop score of 6 favorable?

Under the common convention, no — 6 is the top of the unfavorable range, and NICE recommends cervical ripening for 6 or less. Do not confuse it with the simplified Bishop score, where more than 5 out of 9 is favorable.

What Bishop score do you need for Pitocin?

NICE NG207 offers amniotomy and oxytocin when the score is more than 6, and ripening when it is 6 or less. Some protocols aim for 6 or more in multiparous and 8 or more in nulliparous patients. Follow your unit's protocol.

What is the maximum Bishop score?

13: up to 3 points each for dilation, effacement and station, and up to 2 each for consistency and position.
