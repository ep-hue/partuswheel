How the dates are calculated

Transfer typeDue dateEquivalent LMP
Day-3 embryo transfertransfer + 263 daystransfer − 17 days
Day-5 (blastocyst) transfertransfer + 261 daystransfer − 19 days
Day-6 (blastocyst) transfertransfer + 260 daystransfer − 20 days

Logic: a pregnancy is 266 days from fertilization, and the embryo was already 3, 5 or 6 days old at transfer — so EDD = transfer + (266 − embryo age). The equivalent LMP places fertilization on "cycle day 14", which lets standard 40-week dating work as usual.

Common questions

Why does the transfer day change the due date?

Because the embryo's age is known exactly. A day-5 embryo is two days further along than a day-3 embryo, so the due date lands two days earlier relative to the same transfer date.

Does this work for frozen embryo transfers (FET)?

Yes. What matters is the embryo's developmental age at transfer, not whether it was fresh or frozen. A thawed day-5 blastocyst is dated as day-5.

What about day-6 or day-7 blastocysts?

Day-6 transfers use transfer + 260 days — most calculators skip this option. Day-7 transfers (rare) would follow the same logic at + 259 days; confirm dating with the treating fertility clinic.

Why is IVF dating more accurate than LMP dating?

The fertilization date is known to the day, so there is no recall error and no cycle-length assumption. ACOG treats IVF-derived dating as the most reliable method available.

Can an ultrasound change an IVF due date?

IVF dating is generally kept, because it is more precise than measurement-based estimates. For non-IVF pregnancies, ultrasound may replace LMP dating — check our ACOG redating calculator.
