Common questions

How is the due date calculated?

By Naegele's rule: the estimated due date (EDD) is 280 days — 40 weeks — after the first day of the last menstrual period, assuming a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. From a conception date, the EDD is conception + 266 days.

What if the ultrasound disagrees with these dates?

ACOG defines thresholds for when ultrasound dating should replace LMP dating. Check the difference in our free ACOG redating calculator.

Gestational age vs. fetal age — what's the difference?

Gestational age counts from the LMP; fetal (embryonic) age counts from conception and is about two weeks less. Clinical dating uses gestational age.

What about IVF pregnancies?

IVF pregnancies are dated from the embryo transfer: EDD = transfer date + 263 days (day-3), + 261 days (day-5) or + 260 days (day-6). No LMP is needed. The Partus app computes these directly.

How accurate is a pregnancy wheel?

A physical wheel is read to roughly ±1–2 days because of its printed scale; this calculator does the same arithmetic exactly. Dating accuracy itself depends on how the pregnancy is dated — early ultrasound is accurate to about ±5–7 days, LMP recall varies more.
